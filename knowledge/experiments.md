| 2026-09-26 | Boox Notes gets its ~10 ms ink because SurfaceFlinger draws the stroke itself: its `touchReader`/`touchConsumer` threads read the pen node and push small `HANDWRITE` partial updates to the EPD driver (`/dev/ebc`, root and SurfaceFlinger only). | 2026-09-26 | **The swap is `ENABLE_POST` (16711692: int −1, int enable, int pid).** Sent with 0 at pen-down it holds the app's frames back from the panel, so the app's own drawing can't cut gaps into the preview; sent with 1 after the lift it lets them through and replaces the preview with the app's stroke, while the pen session stays open (pen state still 2). `HANDWRITING_REPAINT` (flag 0 or 1), `SAVE_PEN_ATTACHED_FB`, `REPAINT_EVERY_THING`, the auto-sync switch and the screen-note state pipe (`/dev/onyx/listener`, world-writable) had no visible effect on the preview. The session owner pid can be the app's or BooxUltimatum's own. | Test cells run from the shell while the owner drew in Sketchbook, one call at a time on a stuck preview; `GET_PEN_STATE` after each. | *Instant ink* is back: one open session per app visit, frames held at pen-down and released 500 ms after the lift. |

| 2026-09-26 | EinkWise's third-party handwriting mode (`noteConfig` in the per-app config, used by Boox's presets for OneNote, WPS and others) can be written for any app through `oec_service` (transaction 12, no permission check), but turning it on for Sketchbook (`drawViewKey` `com.adsk.sketchbook.canvas.b`, with and without the activity-level `enable`) gave no `HANDWRITE` updates.
